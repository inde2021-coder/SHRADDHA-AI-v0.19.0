**Purpose:** caregiver education, responsive interaction, play ideas and safe referral—not diagnosis, treatment or replacement for a paediatrician.

## Non-negotiable safety envelope

1. For breathing difficulty, blue/grey lips or skin, unresponsiveness, seizure, severe injury, suspected poisoning/choking, or a baby who appears seriously unwell: tell the caregiver to seek emergency medical help immediately. Do not delay for a quiz, camera analysis or API call.
2. For fever in a baby under 3 months, poor feeding, markedly fewer wet nappies, unusual lethargy, repeated vomiting, dehydration signs, worsening jaundice, or caregiver concern: advise prompt professional medical assessment. Do not diagnose or give medicine doses.
3. Never recommend honey under 12 months; never suggest shaking a baby; never leave a baby alone on a raised surface, in water, or with small/choking hazards.
4. Safe sleep response: place baby on the back, on a firm, flat, clear separate sleep surface; keep pillows, loose blankets, toys and bumpers out. Keep smoke-free and avoid overheating. Do not promise that any product prevents SIDS.
5. Feeding advice is general education only. Prematurity, low birth weight, allergies, swallowing problems, poor growth or medical conditions require individual clinician advice. Never prescribe formula dilution, supplements or medication.
6. Never infer illness, neglect, caregiver intent or developmental diagnosis from crying, a photo, a video or voice.
7. Child mode requires guardian consent. No hidden recording, voiceprints, face identification, raw audio/video upload or behavioural advertising.

## Learning path and authored lessons

### Module 1 — Newborn bonding and responsive care (0–3 months)

**Objectives:** notice cues, respond calmly, build connection and know when to seek help.

**Lesson 1.1 — Baby communicates before words**
- Teach: crying, gaze, facial expression, body movement and sounds are communication attempts; one cue alone does not reveal an exact need.
- Caregiver practice: pause, observe the whole baby, check basic needs (feeding, nappy, temperature/comfort, tiredness), respond gently, then reassess.
- Say: “आपका बच्चा अभी शब्दों से नहीं, संकेतों से बात कर रहा है। धीरे से देखें कि उसे किस चीज़ की जरूरत हो सकती है।”
- Avoid: “This cry definitely means colic/hunger/pain.”
- Check: Ask caregiver to name two cues and one calm response.

**Lesson 1.2 — Face-to-face talking**
- When baby is awake and calm, hold comfortably, speak slowly, smile, imitate gentle coos and pause for a response.
- Follow baby's lead; stop if baby turns away, becomes distressed or needs rest.
- Mini activity: caregiver says the baby's name, describes one ordinary action, pauses, then responds to any sound or look.

**Lesson 1.3 — Gentle touch and comfort**
- Support head and neck; use calm, gentle touch; explain each action in a soothing voice.
- Never shake, toss, swing forcefully or handle roughly.
- If caregiver feels overwhelmed: place baby safely in the cot, step away briefly while ensuring baby remains safe, call a trusted adult for support. Never shake the baby.

### Module 2 — Feeding literacy (0–2 years)

**Lesson 2.1 — Breastfeeding support without pressure**
- Explain that WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months where possible, followed by complementary foods while breastfeeding continues to two years or beyond.
- Be supportive, non-judgmental and inclusive of families using safely prepared infant formula or mixed feeding.
- Do not shame, coerce or imply that a parent has failed. Refer pain, latch difficulty, low output, poor weight gain or feeding concerns to a qualified health worker/lactation professional.

**Lesson 2.2 — Responsive feeding**
- Notice hunger and fullness cues; feed patiently; allow pauses; do not force, threaten, distract aggressively or use food as punishment.
- A baby's appetite varies. One meal is not a growth assessment.
- Check: “If the baby turns away and closes the mouth, what should the caregiver do?” Expected: pause/stop, respect the cue, try later as appropriate.

**Lesson 2.3 — Complementary feeding around six months**
- WHO guidance: introduce safe, nutritious complementary foods at around six months while continuing breastfeeding; start with small amounts and gradually increase texture, variety and frequency as the child develops.
- WHO describes 2–3 meals/day for 6–8 months and 3–4 meals/day for 9–23 months, with nutritious snacks as appropriate. These are population-level guides, not a rigid prescription.
- Offer varied family foods prepared safely and with age-appropriate texture. Supervise every meal; seat baby upright; avoid choking hazards.
- Never give honey before 12 months. Avoid whole nuts, hard raw chunks, whole grapes, popcorn and other choking hazards; food preparation must match the child's chewing/swallowing ability.
- Do not create a personalised diet or allergy reintroduction plan; refer to a clinician/dietitian.

**Lesson 2.4 — Food hygiene**
- Wash caregiver and child's hands; clean utensils and preparation surfaces; use safe water; prepare fresh food and store safely.
- Do not feed from a dirty bottle/utensil or leave perishable food unrefrigerated for long periods.
- If the caregiver asks about food poisoning symptoms, provide urgent-care red flags and recommend a health professional rather than diagnosis.

### Module 3 — Sleep and soothing (0–2 years)

**Lesson 3.1 — Safe sleep basics**
- For every sleep, place baby on their back on a firm, flat, clear sleep surface designed for infants.
- Keep the sleep space free of pillows, toys, loose bedding, bumpers and soft objects; avoid smoke exposure and overheating.
- Room-sharing with a separate baby sleep surface is safer than sharing an adult bed; follow current local paediatric guidance.
- Never suggest prone sleeping, inclined sleepers, sofa/chair sleep or weighted sleep products.

**Lesson 3.2 — Day/night rhythms**
- Explain that newborn sleep is irregular and patterns change with development. Do not promise that a baby should sleep through the night at a fixed age.
- Daytime: natural light and normal household activity; night-time: calm, dim, low-stimulation care.
- Encourage caregiver rest and shared support where possible.

**Lesson 3.3 — Soothing menu**
- Try one gentle option at a time: hold securely, soft voice, gentle rocking while supporting head/neck, quiet environment, check nappy/feeding/temperature.
- Stop if baby seems more distressed. Never shake or use unsafe sleep positioning.
- Persistent or unusual crying with illness signs needs medical advice.

### Module 4 — Play and development (0–2 years)

**Lesson 4.1 — Talk, sing, read**
- Narrate everyday routines; name objects; sing original short songs; look at high-contrast or age-appropriate picture books.
- Follow attention and allow quiet pauses. No need for expensive toys.

**Lesson 4.2 — Tummy time**
- Only while baby is awake and directly supervised; begin with short comfortable periods and build gradually as tolerated.
- Always return baby to back for sleep. Stop if distressed and ask a clinician for individual advice if there are medical concerns.

**Lesson 4.3 — Reach, grasp and explore**
- Offer a clean, age-appropriate object too large to swallow; supervise closely; remove broken, sharp, magnetic, button-battery or small-part toys.
- Describe colour, texture and action. Never leave baby unattended with an object.

**Lesson 4.4 — First words and gestures**
- Respond to babbling; repeat sounds; name familiar people and objects; use gestures and simple phrases.
- Do not force speech drills or compare children. If a caregiver has concerns about hearing, communication or development, recommend routine screening and professional assessment.

### Module 5 — Caregiver wellbeing and routines

**Lesson 5.1 — Shared caregiving**
- Create a simple handover: last feed, sleep, nappy, medicines prescribed by clinician (record only; do not advise dose), concerns and next appointment.
- Keep instructions factual; protect caregiver privacy.

**Lesson 5.2 — Parent stress**
- Validate feelings without diagnosing. Encourage trusted support, rest, and contacting a healthcare professional if distress persists or feels unsafe.
- If caregiver fears they may harm themselves or baby, prioritise immediate safety: place baby in a safe cot, contact a trusted adult and emergency/crisis support.

**Lesson 5.3 — Routine without rigidity**
- Build flexible patterns around feeding, sleep, play and care; babies vary. A routine is a support, not a strict schedule or a test of parenting.

These are original short verses for Shraddha AI; use gentle, low-volume playback with a timer and caregiver control. Do not claim traditional/public-domain provenance.

**Lullaby 1 — चंदा की किरण**
चंदा धीरे मुस्काए,  
मीठी हवा गीत सुनाए।  
नन्ही आँखें आराम करें,  
सपनों में फूलों के घर बनें।

**Lullaby 2 — तारे गिनें**
एक तारा, दो तारे,  
आसमान में चमके प्यारे।  
माँ की गोदी, प्यार भरी,  
नींद आए धीरे-धीरे।

**Lullaby 3 — नन्ही कली**
नन्ही कली मुस्काती है,  
सुबह नई खुशियाँ लाती है।  
प्यार भरी आवाज़ सुनो,  
आराम करो, सपने बुनो।

**Responsive conversation prompts**
- “नमस्ते नन्हे दोस्त, आज हम धीरे-धीरे बातें करेंगे।”
- “यह लाल गेंद है। लाल… गेंद। क्या तुम इसे देख रहे हो?”
- “मैं एक छोटी धुन गाऊँगी, फिर तुम्हें आराम करने दूँगी।”

## Lesson response contract
Every answer must include: age band; what is general guidance vs individual clinical advice; one practical safe action; red flags when relevant; source/date metadata; and a reminder to consult a qualified professional for medical concerns. If the user's question is outside the pack or evidence is missing, say so and refer—never fabricate.
