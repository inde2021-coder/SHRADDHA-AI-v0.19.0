# Shraddha AI Kids Foundation — Lesson Library (Ages 3–10)

**Pack ID:** `kids-foundation-hi-en-3-10` · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** authored starter content, bundled in the APK offline Knowledge Library; educator review and expanded lesson coverage required.
**Approach:** play-based, inclusive, low-pressure learning aligned in spirit with India's Foundational Literacy and Numeracy (FLN) framework. This is not a claimed official NCERT/CBSE/state-board syllabus.

## Teaching contract

- Ask the learner's age band and preferred language through a guardian-managed profile; do not ask for full name, school, address, phone, photo or voiceprint.
- One instruction at a time; short explanations; concrete objects; praise effort and strategy, not fixed intelligence.
- Teaching sequence: ASK → HINT → SIMPLE EXPLANATION → WORKED EXAMPLE → RETRY → REVIEW.
- Never shame, rank publicly, threaten, manipulate, or make the child dependent on the AI. Encourage asking a trusted adult.
- No open web, public chat, purchases, targeted ads, hidden recording or unsupervised links in child mode.
- If the child discloses danger or abuse, respond calmly, do not interrogate, encourage telling a trusted safe adult and follow the product's safeguarding escalation protocol.

## Age band 3–5: play, language and number sense

### Unit 3–5.1 — Listening and speaking
**Goal:** listen to a short prompt, name familiar objects, describe an action.
- Lesson: “Look around. Find something round. Tell me its colour.”
- Model: “A plate is round. The plate is blue.”
- Practice: object naming, sound imitation, two-step familiar instructions.
- Check: child identifies one object and gives one attribute.
- Adaptation: accept pointing, AAC, gesture or home language as valid responses.

### Unit 3–5.2 — Hindi and English sound awareness
- Hindi: सुनो–बोलो–पहचानो; familiar ध्वनियाँ and picture-word matching. Introduce वर्ण recognition through meaningful words, not rote pressure.
- English: initial sound awareness, rhyme, syllable clapping and familiar vocabulary; do not teach confusing letter-name/letter-sound equivalence.
- Practice: identify first sound in “माला”, “कमल”, “sun”, “ball”; accept dialect/accent variation.
- Do not claim phonics mastery from one correct response.

### Unit 3–5.3 — Counting 1–10
- Use real objects: one spoon, two blocks, three leaves.
- Teach one-to-one correspondence: touch/move each object once while counting.
- Exercise: “Put 4 blocks here. Count them aloud.”
- Answer: four objects; if child counts twice, model moving each object into a separate group.

### Unit 3–5.4 — Shapes, colours and patterns
- Identify circle, square, triangle, rectangle using household objects.
- Pattern: red-blue-red-blue; ask what comes next and why.
- Accept multiple valid real-world examples; distinguish shape from object.

### Unit 3–5.5 — Feelings and social skills
- Teach happy, sad, angry, worried, excited and calm as words, not diagnoses.
- Script: “You seem upset. Would you like help, a quiet moment, or to tell me?”
- Practise turn-taking, asking permission, saying stop, seeking help and safe boundaries.

## Age band 6–8: foundational literacy and numeracy

### Unit 6–8.1 — Reading comprehension
**Mini passage:** “रीना के पास एक लाल गेंद है। वह गेंद अपने दोस्त अमन के साथ खेलती है। खेल के बाद दोनों गेंद टोकरी में रखते हैं।”
Questions: (1) गेंद का रंग? (2) रीना किसके साथ खेलती है? (3) खेल के बाद गेंद कहाँ रखी?
Answer key: लाल; अमन; टोकरी में.
Skill: retrieve explicit details, then retell in own words.

### Unit 6–8.2 — Sentence building
- Pattern: who + action + what/where.
- Example: “सीमा किताब पढ़ती है।” / “Ravi waters a plant.”
- Activity: arrange word cards; capital letter/full stop in English; appropriate punctuation in Hindi.
- Feedback: explain one correction at a time.

### Unit 6–8.3 — Place value to 999
- 347 = 3 hundreds + 4 tens + 7 ones.
- Expanded form: 300 + 40 + 7.
- Exercise: write 582 in expanded form. Answer: 500 + 80 + 2.
- Common error: confusing digit value with place value; use bundles of ten.

### Unit 6–8.4 — Addition and subtraction
- Example: 28 + 15: 28 + 10 = 38; +5 = 43.
- Example: 52 − 18: 52 − 10 = 42; −8 = 34.
- Check subtraction by addition: 34 + 18 = 52.
- Use number line, objects and vertical method; teach regrouping conceptually.

### Unit 6–8.5 — Multiplication and division
- Multiplication as equal groups: 3 groups of 4 = 12.
- Division as sharing: 12 objects among 3 groups = 4 each.
- Always check with inverse operation. Avoid memorisation without meaning.

### Unit 6–8.6 — Science observation
- Living/non-living: use observable properties and discuss edge cases carefully.
- Plant needs: light, water, air, suitable soil/space; explain that needs vary by plant.
- Water cycle: evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection, using a labelled simple diagram described in words.
- Teach observation vs inference: “The leaf is wet” (observation); “It rained” (possible explanation).

## Age band 9–10: comprehension, reasoning and projects

### Unit 9–10.1 — Main idea and evidence
Read a short paragraph, identify topic sentence, two supporting details and an inference. Ask “Which words in the passage support your answer?” Distinguish text evidence from personal opinion.

### Unit 9–10.2 — Fractions
- A fraction names equal parts of a whole. In 3/4, 3 is numerator and 4 denominator.
- Compare fractions with same denominator by comparing numerators.
- Equivalent example: 1/2 = 2/4 using equal-sized visual parts.
- Warn that parts must be equal; unequal slices do not represent fourths.

### Unit 9–10.3 — Decimals and money
- ₹12.50 = 12 rupees and 50 paise.
- Align decimal places when adding: 12.50 + 3.25 = ₹15.75.
- Explain that displayed prices, tax and change should be checked; do not execute payments.

### Unit 9–10.4 — Measurement and data
- Estimate then measure length, mass and capacity with appropriate units.
- Record observations in a tally/table; create a bar chart; state what data shows and does not show.
- Never invent missing values.

### Unit 9–10.5 — Scientific method
Question → prediction → fair test → observation/data → conclusion → limitations.
Example: Which paper shape falls more slowly? Change one variable at a time; repeat; discuss why a classroom experiment is not proof of every situation.

### Unit 9–10.6 — Digital citizenship
- Keep passwords private; do not share OTPs, address, school details or images with strangers.
- Ask a guardian before installing apps, clicking unknown links or posting.
- If a message feels frightening or asks for secrecy, stop and tell a trusted adult.

## Cross-age mini-lessons

### English vocabulary: everyday objects
cup, book, door, plant, water, clean, share, help. Teach with a real object, spoken word, sentence and retrieval game.

### Hindi vocabulary: रोज़मर्रा
पानी, किताब, पौधा, दरवाज़ा, साफ़, मदद, दोस्त, परिवार. Use in a meaningful sentence and accept regional language equivalents.

### Environmental learning
Sort clean household examples into reusable, recyclable and waste only where local rules support it. Never ask children to handle broken glass, batteries, chemicals or medical waste.

### Creativity
Draw a new vehicle, describe its purpose, name three parts and explain one safety feature. Multiple answers are welcome.

## Assessment rubric (formative only)
- 0 = not yet attempted; 1 = needs full modelling; 2 = succeeds with hint; 3 = independent explanation.
- Record skill evidence and date, not a permanent label about the child.
- A single quiz is not a diagnosis or school-grade prediction. Progress should include effort, strategy, language and accessibility.

## Original story seed
**The Helpful Neem Tree:** A child notices a young plant drying in the sun. With an adult, they learn to check the soil, water appropriately and protect the plant. Questions: What did the child notice? Why ask an adult? What else does a plant need? Accept reasoned answers; do not over-water every plant.
