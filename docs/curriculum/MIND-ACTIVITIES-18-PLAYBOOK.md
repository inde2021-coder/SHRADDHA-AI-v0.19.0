# Shraddha AI — 18 Mind Activities Playbook

**Pack ID:** `mind-activities-18-hi-en` · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** activity designs, not clinical or IQ tests.

## Universal rules
- Offer age bands 3–5, 6–8, 9–10 and adult; adjust difficulty and accessibility.
- Short, playful rounds; allow breaks and stopping. No scoreboards that shame or compare children.
- Provide visual, spoken, tactile or gesture-based alternatives. Do not require camera or microphone.
- Scores indicate performance in this activity only—not intelligence, ADHD, autism, memory disorder or school readiness.

## Activity cards

1. **Memory Power Games** — show 3–5 safe everyday objects briefly, cover them, ask learner to recall or identify what changed. Increase item count gradually. Skills: recall and attention. Never use private family photos.
2. **Picture Matching** — pair identical or related public-domain/licensed pictures. Start with 3 pairs, increase gradually. Accept spoken names, pointing or touch.
3. **Number Sequence** — complete 1,2,3,? then 2,4,6,? and simple descending patterns. Ask learner to explain the rule; include more than one possible rule where ambiguity exists.
4. **Pattern Recognition** — continue shape/colour/sound patterns (circle-square-circle-?). Use physical objects and explain repeating unit.
5. **Logical Thinking** — simple “which does not belong?” sets; accept multiple defensible answers and ask for reasoning rather than insisting on a single hidden answer.
6. **Shape Puzzle** — combine large safe shapes to make a house/boat. Avoid small pieces for toddlers; adult supervision for physical materials.
7. **Visual Memory** — observe a simple scene, hide it, recall object position. Provide verbal/tactile alternative for low vision.
8. **Listening Activities** — follow one or two familiar instructions; repeat only if requested. Never record the child by default.
9. **Attention Games** — find a target among distractors for a short time; keep calm and non-competitive. Stop if frustration rises.
10. **Sorting and Classification** — sort safe objects by colour, shape, size or use. Explain that an object can belong to multiple groups depending on the rule.
11. **Problem Solving** — give a practical puzzle such as “How can we carry three books safely?” Invite several strategies and discuss safety.
12. **Creative Thinking** — invent a new use for a cardboard box, draw a fantasy garden or create an original story. Reward variety, not conformity.
13. **Observation Games** — describe weather, plant leaves or room objects; separate what is seen from what is guessed. Do not touch unknown plants/animals.
14. **Simple Maze** — guide a character to an exit; offer large-print, high-contrast and spoken directions. No speed requirement.
15. **Story Completion** — provide a beginning and ask learner to invent an ending. Accept multiple endings; avoid frightening or violent prompts.
16. **Find the Difference** — compare two licensed illustrations with 3–5 changes. Use accessible contrast and avoid tiny visual details.
17. **Spatial Awareness** — practise inside/outside, above/below, near/far and rotate simple shapes. Provide hands-on and verbal modes.
18. **Left-Right Recognition** — use learner's own left/right hand, body-safe movement and arrows. Do not assume handedness; allow extra practice without labels.

## Example session
1. Guardian chooses age band and 5–10 minute session limit.
2. Shraddha offers two activities and lets learner choose.
3. Explain one rule; demonstrate one round.
4. Learner tries; give a hint only when requested or stuck.
5. End with one positive observation and one optional next activity.
6. Save minimal progress (activity ID, date, attempts, optional guardian-approved note); no raw audio/video.

## Activity quality tests
- Every task has a clear rule and unambiguous answer OR explicitly accepts multiple valid answers.
- Difficulty increases one dimension at a time.
- Correct answer and explanation are deterministic where applicable.
- No small detachable objects for under-threes; no choking, sharp, toxic, heat or water hazards.
- Accessibility alternative exists; no time pressure required.
- No clinical inference from activity score.
