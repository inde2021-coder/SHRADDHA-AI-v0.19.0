# Shraddha AI — Shloka, Mantra, Katha & Bhagavad Gita Learning Library
Version: 1.0 | Date: 2026-11-26
Status: Authored starter curriculum, bundled in the APK offline Knowledge Library. Sanskrit scholar/teacher review and expanded verse-by-verse source-checked content remain required. Religious practice is personal; the app must remain respectful and non-coercive.

## 1. Learning goals
Shraddha should help a learner:
- read Devanagari and pronounce Sanskrit carefully, with word breaks and optional transliteration;
- understand shloka structure, vocabulary, basic grammar, context and meaning;
- learn selected mantras with source/tradition labels, pronunciation guidance and respectful context;
- hear and read original, age-appropriate kathas, distinguish source text from retelling, and discuss values without claiming a single compulsory interpretation;
- study the Bhagavad Gita chapter by chapter, verse by verse, including Sanskrit, transliteration, word meanings, prose order (anvaya), context, plain-language paraphrase, reflection and practical application;
- compare interpretations from different recognised traditions fairly;
- ask questions in Hindi, English, Sanskrit, Marathi and Pawari where verified translations/content exist;
- learn offline from reviewed packs; use no paid API fallback.

## 2. Content integrity, respect and copyright
- Identify source precisely: Bhagavad Gita chapter/verse; Upanishad section/mantra; named Purana/Itihasa and chapter/episode where known; or oral/community tradition with contributor and region.
- Distinguish original Sanskrit text, transliteration, literal word gloss, prose order, modern paraphrase, commentary and creative retelling. Never blend them without labels.
- Do not invent a verse, mantra, author, source, ritual rule or quotation. If uncertain, say so and queue for review.
- Traditional Sanskrit originals may be public domain, but modern translations, recordings, commentaries, illustrations and performances can be copyrighted. Store only verified public-domain, openly licensed or permissioned material. Write original explanations rather than copying modern translations.
- Gita interpretations differ across traditions. Present a neutral introductory explanation and label named school/commentary perspectives; do not claim one sectarian view is the only meaning.
- Respect Hindu diversity and other faiths/non-religious users. No coercive conversion, caste/communal superiority, hate, threats, shame or claims that illness/disability is caused by insufficient devotion.
- Mantra recitation is optional devotional/cultural learning, not a medical treatment, guaranteed wish-fulfilment, substitute for professional help or a safety intervention.
- Ritual instructions vary by family, region, sampradaya and occasion. Explain variation and recommend a trusted local teacher for practice-specific questions.

## 3. Shloka learning course
### Beginner pathway
1. Devanagari vowels/consonants, conjuncts and vowel marks.
2. Syllable segmentation, anusvara/visarga, long/short vowels and basic pronunciation.
3. Word-by-word reading and sandhi awareness.
4. Common grammatical forms: noun gender/number/case, verb person/number/tense-mood basics.
5. Anvaya: rearranging poetic word order into prose order.
6. Meaning: literal gloss first, then context-sensitive paraphrase.
7. Memorisation by small chunks, repetition and spaced review—never pressure or punishment.
8. Recitation etiquette and audio playback at adjustable speed.

### Demonstration shloka: Bhagavad Gita 2.47
Sanskrit:
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि॥

IAST:
karmaṇy-evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana |
mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo 'stv akarmaṇi ||

Word-gloss (introductory):
- कर्मणि — in action / in the work
- एव — indeed / only (contextual emphasis)
- अधिकारः — scope, rightful domain or responsibility
- ते — your
- मा — do not
- फलेषु — in the results/fruits
- कदाचन — at any time
- अकर्मणि — in inaction

Plain-language teaching paraphrase (not a quotation from a modern translation):
“Give your attention to the action that is yours to do. Do not make the result your sole reason for acting, and do not use uncertainty about results as an excuse for inaction.”
Teaching notes:
- Explain that this is not a promise that effort always produces a desired result.
- It does not mean ignore consequences, abandon planning or avoid responsibility.
- Discuss effort, ethical action, humility about outcomes and learning from feedback.
- Ask learner for a school, farming, sport or household example.
Check: learner explains the difference between responsible effort and obsession with a guaranteed outcome.

### Shloka lesson template
Every lesson contains: source and verse number; script; transliteration; word segmentation; sandhi notes; grammar; anvaya; literal gloss; one or more clearly labelled interpretations; historical/philosophical context; practical reflection; pronunciation audio provenance; 3 comprehension questions; answer rubric; references and reviewer.

## 4. Mantra library and responsible teaching
Build themed, opt-in packs. For every mantra provide the exact source where verified, text, transliteration, pronunciation, literal word meaning, context, tradition/variant notes, audio licence and a note that recitation is a personal choice.

### Starter examples
**A. Om (ॐ / ओम्)**
- Teach as a sacred syllable in several Hindu philosophical and devotional traditions.
- Explain that interpretation varies by text and school; do not reduce all traditions to one definition.
- Practice: listen/read slowly, notice the sound, optional quiet reflection.

**B. Gayatri Mantra — Rigveda 3.62.10**
ॐ भूर्भुवः स्वः।
तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यं भर्गो देवस्य धीमहि।
धियो यो नः प्रचोदयात्॥
- Explain source and the Savitr context carefully.
- Provide word-level grammar only after Sanskrit expert review.
- Do not claim chanting guarantees grades, wealth, health or supernatural outcomes.

**C. Mahamrityunjaya Mantra — traditionally associated with Rigveda 7.59.12**
ॐ त्र्यम्बकं यजामहे सुगन्धिं पुष्टिवर्धनम्।
उर्वारुकमिव बन्धनान्मृत्योर्मुक्षीय मामृतात्॥
- Explain the prayerful imagery and traditional context.
- Never market it as a cure, substitute for treatment or guarantee against death.

**D. Shanti invocation (common form; source and recension must be checked for each version)**
ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै।
तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥
- Teach the themes of shared learning, mutual support and peace.
- Label textual source/recension only after verification; do not attach an uncertain source citation.

**E. “ॐ नमः शिवाय”**
- Teach as a widely used Shaiva devotional mantra.
- Explain word meaning and tradition respectfully; note that practice conventions differ.
- Do not prescribe initiation or ritual requirements as universal facts.

### Mantra categories to author after source verification
- Vedic invocations and selected suktas;
- Upanishadic peace invocations;
- Ganesha, Shiva, Vishnu, Devi, Surya and other devotional traditions;
- nama-japa and simple prayer forms;
- festival/occasion prayers with regional variants.
Each pack must label tradition, source confidence, variant and permissions. Avoid mass scraping audio or lyrics.

The source contains 701 records because it numbers Arjuna's opening question in chapter 13 as 13.1. This build uses the common 700-verse numbering in which that question is not numbered separately and the following verse is 13.1. The source's original reference is retained in each entry as `sourceNumber`; the canonical app reference is retained as `ref`.

**Independent cross-reference:** Hansi Gita Bhawan Trust publishes a 700-verse corpus and chapter counts, and states that its Sanskrit transcription was checked against multiple editions: https://gitabhawan.org.in/gita/research. Its translations are not copied into these packs. The Trust's English/Hindi translations may be used only with its stated attribution and terms; do not silently blend them into the Sanskrit source.

**Review status:** Structural validation confirms 700 unique sequential canonical references and expected counts for all 18 chapters. This is not a substitute for a Sanskrit scholar's line-by-line review. Source transliteration and word glosses are labelled as source-provided, not certified phonetics or authoritative grammar. Padaccheda, anvaya, grammatical analysis, chapter summaries, teaching paraphrases, quizzes and tradition-specific commentaries must be separately authored/reviewed and must cite their reviewer and source. Where editions differ, show the edition/variant rather than silently correcting it.

## 5. Bhagavad Gita — full 18-chapter study programme
Create 18 chapter packs. Each verse entry should have original Sanskrit, IAST, padaccheda where reviewed, word meanings, anvaya, literal gloss, neutral paraphrase, context, reflection, cross-references, quiz and source/reviewer metadata.

Chapter learning map:
1. **Arjuna Vishada Yoga** — moral crisis, grief, confusion, asking for guidance. Discuss emotional distress compassionately; do not shame Arjuna.
2. **Sankhya Yoga** — self, duty, equanimity, disciplined action, knowledge; distinguish philosophical claims from empirical science.
3. **Karma Yoga** — action, responsibility, motives, cooperation and non-attachment to results.
4. **Jnana-Karma-Sannyasa Yoga** — knowledge, action, discernment, tradition and the relationship between wisdom and work.
5. **Karma-Sannyasa Yoga** — renunciation, action and inner discipline; compare interpretations.
6. **Dhyana Yoga** — meditation, attention, self-discipline and moderation. Do not promise medical cures.
7. **Jnana-Vijnana Yoga** — knowledge, experience and the text's account of the divine.
8. **Akshara-Brahma Yoga** — imperishable reality, remembrance and spiritual orientation.
9. **Raja-Vidya-Raja-Guhya Yoga** — devotion, knowledge, accessibility of spiritual practice and divine presence.
10. **Vibhuti Yoga** — divine manifestations and contemplative examples; explain poetic framing.
11. **Vishvarupa-Darshana Yoga** — cosmic vision, awe, scale and Arjuna's response.
12. **Bhakti Yoga** — devotion, qualities of a devoted person, compassion and steadiness.
13. **Kshetra-Kshetrajna-Vibhaga Yoga** — field and knower, body/mind and philosophical analysis; distinguish schools' readings.
14. **Gunatraya-Vibhaga Yoga** — sattva, rajas, tamas as a traditional philosophical framework, not a clinical personality diagnosis.
15. **Purushottama Yoga** — ashvattha imagery, self and the supreme person.
16. **Daivasura-Sampad-Vibhaga Yoga** — constructive and destructive qualities; never label real people or communities as demonic.
17. **Shraddhatraya-Vibhaga Yoga** — faith, dispositions, food/ritual/discipline as presented in the text; contextualise historically.
18. **Moksha-Sannyasa Yoga** — synthesis of knowledge, action, devotion, discernment and liberation; compare commentarial traditions.

### Gita study levels
- Level 1: selected key verses and chapter overview for children/absolute beginners.
- Level 2: all chapter summaries and selected verse vocabulary.
- Level 3: sequential verse study with grammar and reflection.
- Level 4: philosophical concepts and cross-chapter themes.
- Level 5: comparison of recognised commentaries/traditions with citations.
No learner should be told that completing the course makes them spiritually superior.

## 6. Katha and Itihasa/Purana storytelling library
Create source-labelled story packs, including:
- Ramayana episodes and character studies;
- Mahabharata episodes and ethical dilemmas;
- Bhagavata Purana stories (e.g., Prahlada, Dhruva, Krishna childhood episodes) with source/version labels;
- Panchatantra and Hitopadesha stories, distinguishing their literary traditions;
- regional devotional and folk kathas only with community permission and provenance;
- stories of saints and bhakti traditions with historical/legendary details clearly separated.

For every katha:
1. source/version and episode reference;
2. age band and content note;
3. faithful short synopsis;
4. longer retelling labelled as an adaptation;
5. character list and key events;
6. values/questions, not a forced moral;
7. alternate tellings and regional differences;
8. pronunciation guide for names;
9. comprehension questions and activities;
10. copyright/permission record for modern adaptations and audio.

### Sample original teaching retelling (not a source-text quotation): The steady devotee
A child hears the story of Prahlada and asks why he remains calm when others disagree with him. Shraddha explains that many tellings present Prahlada as devoted, courageous and compassionate. She asks: “How can we stay kind and truthful when someone pressures us?” The learner suggests asking a trusted adult for help and refusing to hurt others. The lesson is courage with kindness—not obeying unsafe demands or keeping dangerous secrets.
Safety note: stories must never be used to justify abuse, coercion, discrimination or refusing medical care.

## 7. Cross-language spiritual learning
- Hindi: सरल भावार्थ, शब्दार्थ, कथा-सार, प्रश्नोत्तर and daily-life examples.
- English: plain-language paraphrase, glossary, context and comparative interpretation.
- Sanskrit: original text, sandhi, grammar, anvaya and recitation.
- Marathi: मराठी भावार्थ, शब्दार्थ, कथा, म्हणी/संदर्भ and अभ्यास.
- Pawari: only community-reviewed translations/retellings; if unavailable, show the verified Hindi/Marathi explanation and say Pawari version is not yet reviewed. Never fabricate devotional vocabulary or claim a translation is authentic.
- Translation must preserve source, speaker, metaphor and tradition labels. Verse text is not interchangeable with paraphrase.

## 9. Offline pack and Firebase delivery
- Store text lesson packs in the existing LocalKnowledgeDocumentStore/LocalKnowledgeBase; do not create a second RAG database.
- Separate downloadable modules: Sanskrit basics, shloka collection, mantra collection, Gita 18 chapters, katha collections, Hindi/English/Marathi versions, reviewed Pawari modules.
- Keep large audio files outside APK; download only on request, with licence and size shown.
- Firebase stores account-scoped pack manifests, versions and optional progress sync only after opt-in. Do not upload private voice recordings by default.
- Cache the learner's chosen current pack locally in encrypted storage for airplane-mode access.
- Validate source, licence, checksums, safety and reviewer approval before activation; provide rollback and delete.
- Free API only for online research/translation when enabled and genuinely free; no paid fallback.

## 10. Review and acceptance tests
- Sanskrit scholar reviews text, padaccheda, grammar, anvaya and source references.
- Gita educator/philosophy reviewer checks chapter summaries and interpretive fairness.
- Tradition/community reviewers check devotional context and variants.
- Hindi, English, Marathi translators and Pawari fluent contributors validate language packs.
- Native-speaker review of pronunciation audio and speech recognition.
- Automated tests check verse IDs, exact text integrity, transliteration, chapter sequence, search, citations, offline retrieval and no fabricated references.
- Human evaluation checks that paraphrases are labelled, differing interpretations are represented, and health/financial/legal misinformation is not inferred from scripture.
- Copyright audit for translations, recordings, commentary and artwork.
- Test airplane mode, interrupted downloads, low storage, pack updates, deletion, Firebase opt-in/out and account isolation.

## 11. Starter lesson exercises
1. Read Gita 2.47 and identify the words for action, result and inaction.
2. Explain in your own words why non-attachment is not the same as carelessness.
3. Identify which parts of a lesson are original verse, word gloss and teacher paraphrase.
4. Compare one Gita concept as explained by two clearly named commentarial traditions; list agreement and difference without declaring a winner.
5. Choose a katha and distinguish source episode from the storyteller's added dialogue.
6. Practise one mantra only if you wish; identify its source and one pronunciation feature.
7. Translate a short, reviewed shloka explanation into Hindi, English and Marathi; Pawari only after fluent-speaker review.
8. Write a reflection: “What is one kind and responsible action I can take today?”
