# Shraddha AI Abacus — Eight-Level Course Blueprint with Practice Sets

**Pack ID:** `abacus-foundation-8-levels` · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** structured course blueprint; qualified abacus educator must review bead technique and progression.
**Important:** Abacus is a numeracy practice method. Do not claim guaranteed IQ, memory, school-score or brain-development improvements.

## Teaching principles
- Physical abacus first; explain bead value and place value before speed.
- Accuracy before speed. Every timed drill is optional and age-appropriate.
- Demonstrate → learner moves beads → learner explains → written check → mental visualisation only when ready.
- Confirm the specific abacus model and its bead configuration before teaching. This blueprint assumes a standard soroban-style frame with one upper bead worth five and four lower beads worth one each per rod; other devices may differ.
- For the assumed soroban: each rod is a place value; lower beads count 1 each when moved to the beam; upper bead counts 5 when moved to the beam; beads away from beam are inactive.

## Level 1 — Parts, posture and number representation
**Outcomes:** identify frame, beam, rods and beads; represent 0–9 on one rod.
- Lesson 1: each rod is a place; rightmost rod = ones, next = tens, next = hundreds.
- Lesson 2: show 1–4 with lower beads; show 5 with upper bead; show 6–9 as 5 plus 1–4 lower beads.
- Drill: represent 0, 2, 5, 7, 9; learner says the value before moving to next.
- Check: 8 = upper bead + three lower beads (5 + 3).
- Error correction: count only beads touching the beam; do not count inactive beads.

## Level 2 — Reading numbers and single-digit addition/subtraction
**Outcomes:** read and represent 0–99; perform simple operations where available bead combinations are clear.
- Explain addition as combining quantities and subtraction as removing quantities.
- Practice: 3+2=5; 7−2=5; 4+1=5; 8−3=5.
- Word problem: “There are 4 mangoes and 2 more arrive. How many?” Answer 6.
- Check by inverse operation: 5−2=3.
- Do not teach complement/friend formulas until the educator-approved method is introduced.

## Level 3 — Place value and two-digit operations
**Outcomes:** distinguish tens/ones; read 10–99; add/subtract without regrouping, then with educator-approved complements.
- Represent 34 as 3 tens + 4 ones; 58 as 5 tens + 8 ones.
- Starter drills: 23+14=37; 46−12=34; 31+26=57.
- Explain why 10 ones regroup to 1 ten using bundles/objects before abacus method.
- Regrouping lesson must include a bead-movement diagram validated against the selected abacus method.

## Level 4 — Multi-digit addition and subtraction
**Outcomes:** calculate with hundreds/thousands; understand carrying/borrowing and estimate answers.
- Practice: 125+243=368; 407+186=593; 562−231=331; 700−258=442.
- Teach estimation first: 407+186 is near 400+200=600, so 593 is plausible.
- Verify with inverse operation and written column method.
- Error log: place-value slip, missed bead, carry/borrow error, transcription error.

## Level 5 — Multiplication foundations
**Outcomes:** connect multiplication to equal groups, arrays and place value.
- 4×3 = 4 groups of 3 = 12; draw an array before abacus execution.
- Tables practice: 2, 5, 10, then 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 as learner is ready.
- Practice: 12×3=36; 24×2=48; 15×4=60.
- Check by repeated addition or division.
- Do not teach an unreviewed bead algorithm; include rod allocation diagrams for the exact abacus model.

## Level 6 — Division foundations
**Outcomes:** understand equal sharing, grouping, quotient and remainder.
- 12÷3=4 because 12 objects shared into 3 equal groups gives 4 each.
- Practice: 20÷5=4; 36÷6=6; 25÷4=6 remainder 1.
- Check: divisor × quotient + remainder = dividend; remainder must be smaller than divisor.
- Explain remainder in context; e.g., 25 sweets shared among 4 children leaves 1.

## Level 7 — Mixed operations and mental imagery
**Outcomes:** choose operation, solve multi-step tasks, visualise only after physical fluency.
- Order: understand the question → identify operations → estimate → calculate → check.
- Practice: (18+12)−9=21; 6×4+5=29; 48÷6+7=15.
- Mental imagery is optional. Learner may return to physical beads at any time.
- Use short sessions and breaks; never frame slower speed as failure.

## Level 8 — Application and mastery portfolio
**Outcomes:** apply numeracy to money, measurement, inventory and word problems.
- Money: 3 items at ₹25 each cost ₹75; change from ₹100 is ₹25.
- Inventory: 48 chairs, 15 dispatched, 9 returned; available count = 42 if all counts refer to same stock and no other movement occurred.
- Measurement: 4 strips of 25 cm total 100 cm.
- Mastery portfolio: accuracy, explanation, strategy, self-correction and confidence; speed is secondary.

## Test design
- Separate concept checks from speed rounds.
- Randomise practice order but preserve a known answer key.
- Test boundary cases: zero, crossing ten, crossing hundred, subtraction requiring regrouping, remainder.
- Property-based test: addition commutative; subtraction generally not; multiplication commutative; division check using divisor × quotient + remainder.
- Keep per-question steps so incorrect answers can be explained, not merely marked wrong.
